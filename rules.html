Roam Across the World: rules and guide

You start a campaign in Trafalgar Square with £250. Each leg takes you to a random city 1,000 to 2,000 km away. Explore at your own pace, manage your money, and keep a journal of your travels.

Planning

When a leg starts you see the destination's name. Tap the map to drop a pin where you think the city is, then lock in your guess. The further off you are, the more time you lose before you set off.

Choosing a route

A short overview introduces the kinds of connections available for your journey. Then choose individual services as you go; fares and departure times are shown at each stop. You can switch between stations, bus stations, ferry terminals and airports. The target time is based on reasonable journeys and earns you a bonus if you arrive in time.

Travelling

Choose where to go next from the options at each stop. Trains, buses and ferries leave at set times, shown on each option as the day and time you leave and arrive. Looking for routes costs time but reveals more options.

Evening exploring

Before you book a room, you can explore the town until 9pm. This uses no time. You can look at the market, the things to do and the paintings nearby, then return to the sleeping options.

Overnight travel

If you have to wait more than four hours and the wait is still going at 3 am, you need somewhere to sleep. Services that leave before then have already gone, and morning services have not started yet. Pick a room in town, or spend the night at the station, which is free but leaves you tired and costs an hour on the next leg. Some long-distance buses and trains run through the night. If you take one, you sleep on board and no hotel is needed. Shared minibuses and taxis leave when full, so they never need a night's stay.

City Timeline

Each town has a ten-event timeline about its history. Place each card where it belongs. A card placed right first time is worth 20 points. After one wrong guess it is worth 10, after two 5, after three 2, and 1 from then on. At the end your points are paid as pounds for that town. You can earn from each town once per leg. It costs no time.

Travel events

Most stops go to plan. Each stop rolls a twelve-sided die. A 1 is a breakdown (a train or bus), a mechanical failure (a ferry) or a technical fault (a flight), and adds 20% to that leg's time. A 2 is bad weather, adding 10% to the time. A 3 is a problem with your papers when you cross a border: a fee of half the fare, or 10% more time on a free leg. Elsewhere a 3 is uneventful. A 4 or 5 is a slight delay of 3 to 5%. A 6 is a quicker run, saving 5%. On 7 to 12 nothing goes wrong, and you get a short note about the journey.

Within one leg, at most one serious problem can happen. Any further one is only a slight delay.

Running out of money

If you cannot afford any onward fare and no checkpoint work is open to you, your leg ends where you are. You spend a rest day in that town, and you can sell what you carry at the market. The next income is £50, paid when you set off, the same as a normal leg. Flights and fast trains may be out of reach for a while. The campaign does not end when you run out of money.

Services leave only within their operating hours, and a service that is due outside those hours waits until it reopens.

Target and bonus

Arrive by the target time, counting planning, travel and the checkpoint, and you earn a £150 bonus. Money you keep carries over into the next leg.

Checkpoint

At the destination you find the exact spot from a photo, using the map. Your distance off costs time: nothing within 100 m. Beyond that the time lost rises steeply, about 2 hours at 500 m, 3½ hours at 1 km and 5 hours at 2 km, levelling off near 6 hours from about 4 km.

The photos and their locations used at checkpoints are taken from Wikipedia. The geolocation of a photo may differ from the place's position on the map, so the photo can be a little away from where the pin is.

Interim photo stops

At a stop on your journey, except your destination, you can optionally stop for photo work when your available money is below £100. The game will let you know when funds are running low. It costs two hours, whatever the result. You earn £100 for a spot-on pin, falling to £50 at 2 km out. You can do this once per visit, so returning to a town later allows another go. Missing the target time is the penalty for stopping.

Income

At the start of each new leg you receive £50, paid when you set off at 8 am on the morning after your rest day. It is added to the money you carry over. Keep your money growing and you can keep going: the campaign is open-ended. On a rest day, you can visit the local market, buy goods and find buyers in later towns; trading is optional.

Saving and importing

Your campaign is saved automatically in the browser you are playing in. To keep a copy or carry on elsewhere, open the Settings cog and choose Download save file. This downloads a .json file to your device. Keep it somewhere safe, such as Google Drive.

To carry on, choose Load save file or code on the home page to import a downloaded save or enter your four-word code. You can also enter a code in the Settings modal to load a cloud save. Your campaign and settings are restored from either source.

You can turn cloud saving off in Settings. This deletes the cloud copy and removes the save code from this device. Your campaign stays saved in this browser; download a save file to move it manually. Save-code and personal-link options are hidden while cloud saving is off. This setting applies per device: turn it off on each device, since another device that remains enabled can create a new cloud copy.

To end a campaign, choose End campaign in Settings. This clears the local save; choose Delete cloud save separately if you also want to remove the cloud copy.

Settings, including your things to do links, are saved separately in each browser and are also included in every save file. Importing a file replaces the campaign you are playing in this browser, and replaces your settings with the ones in the file.

Free, and private

Roam Across the World is free to play. It has no accounts, adverts, analytics or trackers, and the game itself sets no cookies. Your campaign, settings and tour records are kept in this browser's local storage.

Cloud saving is optional. When enabled, a copy of your campaign and settings is sent to the game's Cloudflare save service, stored under a hash of your four-word save code, and expires 60 days after its last update. Cloudflare keeps standard request logs. The code is not tied to an account, but anyone who has it can access or overwrite that cloud save.

Turning cloud saving off deletes the cloud copy and removes the code from this device. Local browser saves and downloaded save files remain available. This setting applies per device, so another device with cloud saving enabled can create a new cloud copy. Clearing this browser's site data removes its local progress and stored code; an existing cloud copy remains for up to 60 days and requires its code or personal link to restore.

To load what it needs, the game asks some outside services: map tiles, Wikipedia for photo locations, Open-Meteo for the weather, and an AI model, for the City Timeline's events. Those requests are made like any website's, so the services receive the information the request contains, such as the place you are looking at.
